Using H.E.R.D.

A practical guide to running HERD, understanding its models, reading the performance metrics, and choosing a decision threshold for host-aware protein immunogenicity prediction.

01 · Overview

What HERD predicts

HERD (Host-aware Estimation of antigen immunogenicity and Ranking through Deep-learning) predicts, for a given protein and a chosen host species, the probability that the protein is immunogenic in that host. Unlike host-agnostic tools that assign one score per protein, HERD models immunogenicity as a protein-intrinsic signal shaped by the host in which it is evaluated.

It is trained on curated, protein-level, host-resolved immunogenicity data (manual literature curation + IEDB B-cell records), with length-matched, homology-filtered negatives so the classifier cannot exploit trivial sequence cues. Predictions can be used to prioritise and rank antigen candidates for reverse-vaccinology pipelines across veterinary and human hosts.

Web tool — step by step

  1. Open the immunoHERD Space on Hugging Face.
  2. Paste one or more protein sequences in FASTA format (or upload a FASTA file).
  3. Select the host species whose immune context you want to model.
  4. Run the prediction and read the immunogenicity probability returned for each sequence.
  5. Rank your candidates by score, or apply a cutoff (see Decision threshold).

03 · Input & output

What goes in, what comes out

Input

  • Format: protein sequences in FASTA (amino-acid, single-letter).
  • Length: sequences shorter than 40 residues are outside the training regime; sequences longer than 1,022 residues are truncated before encoding.
  • Residues: non-canonical residues / chemical modifications are not supported.

Output

  • A probability in [0, 1] per protein for the selected host — higher means more likely immunogenic.
  • Use the raw score to rank candidates, or a threshold to get a binary immunogenic / non-immunogenic call.

04 · The models

How the models are built

Every protein is first turned into features by a protein language model; those features then feed the predictors.

Protein representation — ESM-2

Each sequence is encoded with ESM-2 (esm2_t33_650M_UR50D, 650M parameters, 33 layers). The final-layer per-residue vectors are mean-pooled into a single 1,280-dimensional embedding per protein, which is the input to all downstream models.

Individual (host-specific) models

One model per core host: a multilayer perceptron over the 1,280-D embedding — a normalization layer, three fully connected layers (1,024 → 256 → 64 ReLU units, dropout after the first two) and a sigmoid output. Trained with Adam + binary cross-entropy, class weighting, and early stopping; dropout and learning rate tuned with Optuna (objective: validation AUROC).

Integrated (pan-species) model

A single network with a shared trunk (1,024–256–64) feeding one sigmoid head per host; the host identity selects its head. Joint training lets hosts share cross-species immunogenicity signal while keeping host-specific decision functions. It is trained on 15 hosts — the 6 core hosts plus 9 additional IEDB-only species — with a tunable per-host weighting so large datasets do not dominate.

Recommended: the integrated model matched or improved every core host versus its individual model (no AUROC decrease), so it is the default choice. The largest gain was for the hardest host, Bos taurus (AUROC 0.776 → 0.826).

Auxiliary models (the 9 additional heads)

Nine extra species were mined from IEDB and added primarily to broaden the shared antigen repertoire seen by the trunk — they were not used for model selection, hence "auxiliary". In practice their heads still classify accurately (AUROC 0.86–0.96), so several are usable predictors in their own right for additional hosts of applied interest (e.g. Capra hircus 0.900, Ovis aries 0.859).

06 · Performance

Discrimination is threshold-independent (AUROC, AUPRC); at the default 0.5 threshold we also report precision, recall, F1, Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC) and balanced accuracy. All values are pooled out-of-fold predictions from homology-aware nested cross-validation.

Recovery of licensed vaccine antigens

As a real-world positive control, the final pan-species model scored 24 antigens from licensed/commercial vaccines across the six core hosts. HERD recovered 23 of 24 (95.8%) as immunogenic, mean predicted probability 0.914. Per-host recovery was 100% for all hosts except Canis (4/5). The single miss was Protein Q of LETIFEND (0.378), an engineered maltose-binding-protein fusion outside the natural antigen sequence space.

07 · Decision threshold

Choosing & adjusting the threshold

HERD returns a probability, not a hard label. The reported classification metrics use a default decision threshold of 0.5: scores above are called immunogenic, below non-immunogenic. AUROC and AUPRC do not depend on this choice.

How to move it

  • Raise the threshold (e.g. 0.7–0.9) → higher precision, fewer false positives. Best when you can only take a short list of candidates forward to the lab.
  • Lower the threshold → higher recall, fewer missed immunogens, at the cost of more false positives.
  • Just rank — for prioritisation you often don't need a cutoff at all; sort candidates by score and take the top N.

08 · Interpretation

Reading a score & caveats

  • A score is the estimated probability of humoral immunogenicity in the selected host — the principal correlate of protection for most licensed vaccines, and for T-dependent protein antigens it generally implies concomitant CD4⁺ T-helper recognition.
  • Immunogenicity ≠ protection. A high score flags a promising candidate, not a guaranteed protective antigen.
  • Host matters: the same protein can score differently across hosts by design. Pick the host that matches your application.
  • Engineered / chimeric sequences (fusion constructs, artificial linkers) may fall outside the natural antigen space and score low even when protective (e.g. the LETIFEND Protein Q fusion).
